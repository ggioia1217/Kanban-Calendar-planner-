# ADHD Research Foundations

Only peer-reviewed research or work by clinical psychologists/psychiatrists is used here.
Each finding maps to a feature. **Evidence strength** is noted honestly — some features are
reasonable design choices based on the research, not directly proven by it.

> This is not medical advice and the app should never claim to treat ADHD. App Store and FTC rules
> also restrict health claims; marketing must say "designed with ADHD research in mind", not
> "clinically proven", unless we run our own trial.

---

## 1. Time perception differs in ADHD → visual pie timer, no raw minutes on cards
- **Zheng, Q. et al. (2020/2022).** *Time Perception Deficits in Children and Adolescents with ADHD:
  A Meta-analysis.* Journal of Attention Disorders, 26(2). 1,620 participants with ADHD vs 1,249
  controls: less accurate (g > 0.40) and less precise (g = 0.66) time perception, tendency to
  overestimate. https://www.caddra.ca/time-perception-deficits-in-children-and-adolescents-with-adhd-a-meta-analysis/
- **Marx, I. et al. (2021).** Meta-analysis of 55 studies (J. Am. Acad. Child Adolesc. Psychiatry):
  broad timing deficits including time estimation and production.
  https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1527936/meta-analysis-altered-perceptual-timing-abilities-
- **Barkley, R. A. (1997).** *ADHD, self-regulation, and time: toward a more comprehensive theory.*
  Psychological Bulletin. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9276836/
- **Feature:** filling pie timer; estimates hidden on cards; 1× / 2× estimate check-ins.
- Strength: strong evidence for the deficit; the visual-timer *fix* is a reasonable design inference.

## 2. "Externalize" and act at the point of performance → cards, next step, widgets, cues
- **Barkley's clinical principles:** ADHD is a problem of *doing* what you know rather than *knowing*
  what to do. Effective supports externalize information and time and sit at the point of
  performance. https://sciences.ucf.edu/psychology/childrenslearningclinic/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2013/08/Barkley-1997-Psych-Bulletin.pdf
- **Feature:** next step on every card; Now mode; widgets/Live Activity; haptic/visual cues.
- Strength: well-established clinical framework; app-specific outcomes not tested.

## 3. Structured time-management & planning skills work → the whole product's backbone
- **Solanto, M. V. et al. (2010).** *Efficacy of Meta-Cognitive Therapy for Adult ADHD.* Am J
  Psychiatry 167(8):958–68. RCT, n = 88. Group training in **time management, organization and
  planning** beat a supportive-therapy control on ADHD symptoms.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20231319/
- **Safren, S. A. et al. (2010).** *CBT vs Relaxation with Educational Support for Medication-Treated
  Adults with ADHD.* JAMA 304(8):875–880. RCT, n = 86. 67% vs 33% responders; gains held at 6–12
  months. CBT modules include organizing/planning and breaking tasks into steps.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3641654
- **Feature:** goal → task → steps breakdown; daily plan ritual; done-when; rollover prompts.
- Strength: strong (RCTs), but these are therapist-led programs — the app borrows the skills,
  it doesn't replace therapy.

## 4. If-then plans boost follow-through → "When/where" cues on routines
- **Gollwitzer, P. M. & Sheeran, P. (2006).** Meta-analysis, 94 tests, >8,000 participants:
  implementation intentions ("If it's 9am Monday, then I call vendors") improve goal attainment,
  d = 0.65; effects stronger when self-regulation is hard.
  https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes
- **Gawrilow, C. & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2008).** Implementation intentions improved response
  inhibition in children with ADHD, to the level of controls. Cognitive Therapy and Research
  32:261–280. https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-facilitate-response-inhibition-adhd-children
- **Feature:** routines are phrased and scheduled as if-then cues ("After my 9am coffee → follow-ups");
  goal coach writes routines in if-then form.
- Strength: strong in general population; promising but smaller studies in ADHD (mostly children).

## 5. Immediate rewards matter more in ADHD → reward every step
- **Luman, M., Oosterlaan, J. & Sergeant, J. A. (2005).** Review of 22 studies (n = 1,181):
  reinforcement improves performance and motivation, somewhat more in ADHD; strong preference for
  immediate over delayed rewards. Clin Psychol Rev 25(2):183–213.
  https://core.ac.uk/download/303622637.pdf
- **Sonuga-Barke, E. J. S. (2002).** Dual pathway model — *delay aversion* is a core motivational
  feature of ADHD. Behav Brain Res 130:29–36. https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/18277
- **Feature:** micro-celebrations on every step; visible "next milestone"; widget check-offs.
- Strength: good evidence (mostly children); specific gamification designs remain untested.

## 6. Habits take ~2 months and missing a day is OK → forgiving streaks, milestone pacing
- **Lally, P. et al. (2010).** *How are habits formed.* Eur J Soc Psychol 40:998–1009. n = 96;
  median ~66 days to automaticity (range 18–254); **missing one day did not derail habit
  formation**; simpler behaviors became automatic faster.
  https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit
- **Feature:** forgiving streaks; goal coach sets expectations ("about 2 months, start small");
  milestones at 10/25/50/100 align with this timeline.
- Strength: solid general-population study; not ADHD-specific.

## 7. Movement breaks help → walk / stretch / yoga suggestions
- **Cerrillo-Urbina, A. J. et al. (2015).** Meta-analysis, 8 trials, 249 children: aerobic exercise
  moderately-to-largely improved attention (SMD 0.84), hyperactivity and impulsivity; yoga also
  showed benefits. Child Care Health Dev 41(6):779–788. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25988743/
- **Caveat:** later reviewers note it included non-randomized studies, which may inflate effects.
- **Feature:** break suggestions every ~90 min; short movement options.
- Strength: moderate; child-focused.

## 8. Body clock is often later in ADHD → personal fresh window, not a fixed "morning"
- **Bijlenga, D., Vollebregt, M. A., Kooij, J. J. S. & Arns, M. (2019).** Delayed sleep phase found
  in 73–78% of children and adults with ADHD. ADHD Atten Def Hyp Disord 11:5–19.
  https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12402-018-0271-z
- **Coogan, A. N. & McGowan, N. M. (2017).** Systematic review: ADHD associated with later
  chronotype / eveningness. https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/11298/1/AC-Systematic-2017.pdf
- **Feature:** fresh window set by the user (default 9am–1pm for you), optional medication offset,
  learned adjustment — never assume early morning is best.
- Strength: strong for the phenomenon.

## 9. CBT-informed apps can help adults with ADHD → proof the category works
- **Knouse, L. E. et al. (2022).** *Inflow* app feasibility study, PLOS Digital Health: good
  usability; majority of 7-week users reported fewer symptoms (uncontrolled).
  https://doaj.org/article/fc1e182fbb5b4f1e891ba84c54821ac2
- **Antshel, McBride & Knouse (2025).** RCT, n = 154 adults: 8 weeks of a CBT-informed app reduced
  inattentive and hyperactive symptoms vs waitlist. J Atten Disord.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/10870547251384462
- Strength: emerging; supports the idea that a well-designed app can help.

## Known gaps (features we're building on judgment, not proof)
- Card "shake" animations, themes, body-doubling, sound design — sensible but untested; we'll use
  in-app A/B testing and user interviews.
- Over-stimulation risk: every cue has an off switch.

## Rules for the AI "evidence card" feature
1. Cite only peer-reviewed studies, systematic reviews, or guidelines from medical/psychological
   bodies.
2. Show the strength of evidence (meta-analysis > RCT > observational > expert opinion).
3. Never fabricate a citation; if nothing solid exists, say "limited evidence".
4. Link to the source (PubMed/DOI) so users can verify.
