# ADHD Research Update: Newest Findings and Overlooked Older Ones

Researched Nov 2026. Builds on `02-adhd-research.md` (time perception, Barkley, implementation intentions,
immediate rewards, habits, exercise, circadian rhythm) and `05-market-and-research-notes.md`.
Evidence levels: **Strong** (meta-analysis / large RCT pool) · **Some** (single trial, small or observational) · **Early** (case series, pilot).
Cueue supports daily functioning. It is **not a treatment** and shouldn't claim to be.

---

## A. Newest research (2024–2026)

### 1. What actually changes ADHD symptoms in adults
- **Ostinelli et al., Lancet Psychiatry, Jan 2025:** network meta-analysis of 113 trials (14,887 adults). Only
  stimulants and atomoxetine reliably reduced core symptoms in the short term. Non-drug treatments gave
  inconsistent results across raters, and few trials followed people beyond 26 weeks. **Strong**
- **Non-drug-only network meta-analysis, Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2025** (37 trials, 2,289 adults): CBT ranked best,
  especially with anxiety or depression alongside ADHD; mindfulness-based CBT for those without. **Some–Strong**
- **App implication:** be honest in marketing ("helps you plan and follow through", not "treats ADHD"). Lean into
  **CBT-style skills** (planning, breaking tasks down, reframing unhelpful thoughts) and keep the
  **medication-timing** option for fresh hours, since medication is what most reliably changes symptoms.

### 2. Mindfulness
- Meta-analyses (2020; Oliva et al. 2024; Kim & Jung 2025) show **modest** improvements in core symptoms and daily
  functioning versus waitlist, with small trials and some publication bias. **Some**
- **App implication:** keep "Meditation" as a break option; offer 3–10 minute guided options rather than long sessions.

### 3. White and pink noise
- **Nigg et al., JAACAP 2024** meta-analysis: a **small benefit** for people with ADHD (g ≈ 0.25), but a **small harm**
  for people without ADHD (g ≈ −0.21). Brown noise hasn't been studied. Controls were often weak. **Some**
- **App implication:** an optional **focus sound** (white or pink) in Now mode, **off by default**, with a
  one-week "is this helping?" check-in. Never turn it on for a whole team or family.

### 4. Procrastination is about motivation, not just mood
- **Netzer Turgeman & Pollak, J Atten Disord 2026** (640 adults, preregistered): motivation factors explained the
  ADHD–procrastination link; emotion-regulation strategies did not once motivation was accounted for. **Some**
- **2023 temporal-motivation study:** ADHD symptoms went with more task aversiveness, more impulsiveness and
  **lower expectancy of success**. **Some**
- **App implication:**
  - raise **expectancy**: "You've done this 12 times", a tiny first step, a "Start for 5 minutes" button
  - make **value** visible: show the goal's "why" on the task card
  - shrink **delay**: rewards per step (already in)
  - reduce **aversiveness**: "Pair it with…" (temptation bundling) and body-doubling experiments

### 5. Exercise right before demanding work
- **2025 meta-analysis (J Global Health):** a single bout of exercise improved inhibitory control in adults with
  ADHD. **2024 systematic review:** small positive effect, low certainty; sessions over 30 minutes showed
  larger gains. **Some**
- **App implication:** suggest a **movement primer** (a 10–20 minute walk or workout) before the fresh-hours block
  or a Heavy task. Optional.

### 6. Hormones and ADHD in women
- **de Jong et al., 2023** (case series, 9 women): symptoms and mood worsened premenstrually; adjusted treatment
  helped. **Kooij, de Jong et al., 2025 consensus:** hormonal transitions worsen symptoms and mood, yet tailored
  tools are lacking. **Early**
- **App implication:** **opt-in, private, on-device** cycle-aware planning. In the premenstrual week, suggest
  lighter days, more buffer and fewer Heavy tasks. Never shared with employers or teams. Easy to turn off and delete.

### 7. Remembering to do things later (prospective memory)
- **Altgassen et al., 2019 (Springer):** adults with ADHD carried out fewer of their own real-life intentions, and
  this helped explain procrastination. **Time-based** reminders ("at 3pm") are consistently harder than
  **event-based** ones ("after the meeting"). Highly visible cues help. **Some**
  (CHADD 2025 practitioner guidance agrees: turn time cues into event cues.)
- **App implication:** **event-based reminders**:
  - "when my 2pm meeting ends"
  - "after I finish task X"
  - "when I get to work" (location, opt-in)
  - "when I open the app tomorrow"

### 8. Hyperfocus
- **Hupfeld, Abagis & Shah, 2019** (two samples, 623 adults): more ADHD symptoms meant more frequent hyperfocus,
  meaning intense focus, losing track of time and neglecting needs. A 12-item version of the questionnaire was
  validated in 2024. **Some**
- **App implication:**
  - a **hyperfocus guard**: when you're deep in a task past its estimate, gentle "water? food? anything due?"
    nudges instead of urgency alarms
  - an option to **protect a hyperfocus window** so it isn't interrupted for low-priority items
  - always warn before a hard commitment, such as a meeting starting soon

## B. Older research that's still valid but gets less attention

### 9. Emotional ups and downs are part of ADHD
- **Beheshti et al., 2020 meta-analysis:** adults with ADHD showed much higher emotion dysregulation than controls
  (g ≈ 1.17), and it tracked symptom severity. About **70%** of adult patients report it. A 2023 systematic review
  agrees it behaves like a core symptom. **Strong**
- **App implication:**
  - shame-free language everywhere
  - a **"Rough day" mode**: one tap trims today to essentials, moves the rest without penalty and softens cues
  - recovery after missed days with no catch-up pile
  - an optional quick mood check-in that adjusts the plan

### 10. Rewards now beat rewards later (delay discounting)
- **Jackson & MacKillop, 2016 meta-analysis** (25 comparisons, 3,913 people): people with ADHD discount future
  rewards more steeply (d ≈ 0.43), regardless of age or reward type. **Strong**
- **App implication:** keep instant rewards; **bring future payoffs into the present** with progress bars toward
  the long goal on every routine card, "3 more to your badge", and weekly progress highlights.

### 11. Spontaneous mind-wandering
- **Seli et al., 2015** (over 1,350 people plus a clinical sample): **spontaneous** (unintended) mind-wandering is
  strongly tied to ADHD; deliberate daydreaming isn't. **Some–Strong**
- **App implication:** a **"Later" parking lot** on the Now card. Jot a stray thought or task in two taps without
  leaving the current task; it lands in the Inbox to sort afterwards.

### 12. Movement can help thinking
- **Sarver et al., 2015; Kofler, Sarver & Wells, 2015:** in children with ADHD, more movement went with **better**
  working-memory performance, the opposite of typical kids, and working-memory demands caused more movement.
  Small studies, children. **Some**
- **App implication:** don't design for stillness. Mark tasks that can be done standing or walking (walk-and-talk
  calls), suggest a fidget or standing break during long Heavy tasks, and celebrate movement breaks.

### 13. Time in nature
- **Faber Taylor & Kuo, 2009** (17 children, randomized order): concentration was better after a 20-minute park
  walk than after urban walks (d ≈ 0.52–0.77). Small and child-only, but replicated in related work. **Some**
- **App implication:** a **"Green break"** option ("walk somewhere with trees for 15–20 minutes"),
  suggested before demanding work when the weather and calendar allow.

### 14. Fresh starts
- **Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014:** people are more likely to start goals after **temporal landmarks** (new week,
  month, birthday, holiday). The effect is short-lived but useful. Not ADHD-specific. **Some–Strong**
- **App implication:**
  - **fresh-start prompts** on Mondays, the 1st of the month, birthdays, and after time away
    ("New week, clean slate: pick one routine to restart")
  - a Monday or Sunday weekly review
  - never show a backlog of "missed" items on a fresh start

---

## C. Recommended additions (in order)
1. **"Later" parking lot** on the Now card (spontaneous mind-wandering)
2. **Event-based reminders**: after a meeting, after a task, on arrival, on next open (prospective memory)
3. **Motivation boosters**: "Start for 5 minutes", "You've done this N times", the goal's "why" on cards (procrastination research)
4. **Rough-day mode** and shame-free recovery (emotion dysregulation)
5. **Hyperfocus guard** (needs check-ins and protected windows)
6. **Movement primer and Green break** before demanding work (acute exercise, nature, movement)
7. **Fresh-start prompts** and a weekly review (fresh start effect)
8. **Optional focus sounds** (white/pink noise), off by default
9. **Opt-in, private cycle-aware planning** (hormones)
