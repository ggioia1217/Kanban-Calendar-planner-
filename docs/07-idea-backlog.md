## Family photo cleanup game (possible separate app)
**The problem:** phones fill up with screenshots and near-duplicate photos, and sorting them is
exactly the kind of boring, open-ended job that gets put off.

**The idea:** turn cleanup into a short family game.
- Pulls in screenshots and photos, a small batch at a time.
- Family members vote to keep a photo or free up the space. Duplicates and blurry shots are grouped
  so one vote clears a set.
- For photos worth keeping, people add the memory: who is in it, what was happening, and why it
  matters. Those notes travel with the photo.
- Points and streaks for sorting, and a running total of storage freed.
- Kept photos with stories become a shared family memory album.

**Open questions:** whether it lives inside Cueue (as a family routine) or stands alone; how voting
works when people disagree; privacy for photos of children; and how photo-library access differs
between iPhone and Android.

## Battle mode: tasks as monsters (Cueue theme)
**The idea:** an optional theme layered on top of how Cueue already works, turning the day into a
turn-based strategy fight. The work is the bad guy, and every bit of progress is a hit.

- **Monsters = tasks.** A task's size sets the monster's health. Bigger tasks (longer estimates,
  more steps) are bigger monsters that take more hits.
- **Abilities = steps.** Checking off a step is using an ability: it does damage, with a hit
  animation and sound. Harder steps hit harder. "Complete" is the finishing move.
- **Bosses = goals and long projects.** A project's sub-goals and tasks are the **minions** along
  the way; the boss can only be fought once enough minions are down. Flow stages could be the
  boss's phases.
- **Story:** each project gets a short narrative (where the hero is, what's at stake), told in
  small beats as minions fall. The final win gets its own victory scene and a trophy.
- **Ties to what exists:**
  - effort points become damage
  - the treat jar becomes loot
  - stickers become trophies
  - a light day is resting at the inn, with no fights and no penalty
  - a roadblock is a monster's shield that needs an ally (the person you're waiting on)
  - shared team flows are a raid where everyone hits the same boss
- **Why it could work:** in the gamification meta-analysis (Sailer & Homner 2020), adding game
  fiction (a story) and mixing competition with teamwork were the most effective elements for
  changing behavior.
- **Watch-outs:**
  - Keep it optional and never punishing: no losing health, and no monsters "winning" when a task
    moves.
  - The art, characters and world must be our own, not borrowed from an existing game.
  - Missed days don't heal the monster. Progress always stays.

## Coach and clinician summary (Cueue feature)
**The idea:** a one-page report the person chooses to share before an appointment with a doctor,
therapist or ADHD coach, so "How have the last few weeks been?" has a real answer.

- **For a clinician:**
  - focus window vs. when meds were taken
  - energy and sleep trends from check-ins
  - function: tasks finished, how often things got pushed, how often the shield caught them
  - the person's own notes
- **For a coach:**
  - routines that stuck or slipped
  - patterns in pushed tasks
  - when-then plans kept vs. skipped
  - weekly review results and goal progress
- **Sharing:**
  - the person picks what's included
  - a PDF or an expiring private link, never a live feed
  - never shared with an employer
  - describes patterns, never diagnoses or dose advice
- **Start simple:** a "Share a summary" button that makes a PDF. Clinic partnerships later (which
  bring HIPAA-type rules).
